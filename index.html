How the engine decides

Step 1 · Filter

Can it fulfill the order?

  • Capacity and lead time
  • Distance and delivery window
  • Dietary fit and minimum order value
Step 2 · Rank

Will it go well?

  • Team history and similar teams
  • On time rate, issue rate, review trend
  • Budget fit and variety
Step 3 · Balance

Safe or new?

  • Two proven picks, one exploratory
  • Lean familiar for client meetings
  • New caterers earn slots through quality gates
A $20 per person budget: hit $20, or protect the minimum?
  • Budget is a ceiling, not a target. I would not push prices up.
  • Caterers that clear their minimum naturally rank first. Twelve people at $20 is $240, which clears a $200 minimum.
  • If an order falls short, show it openly: Add $30 to meet the minimum, with sides or drinks suggested. No silent upsell, and fulfillment success is a guardrail.

Sequenced roadmap

Three phases, each earning the next.

1

Eligibility and trust

Problem
Recommending a caterer that cannot fulfill the order is worse than recommending nothing.
Dependency
Reliable availability, capacity, and dietary data. Order to delivery event tracking.
Experiment
Eligibility filters and a reliability gate versus current results, with a team level holdout.
Success threshold
Issue and cancellation rates down 10% relative, with completed orders per team not lower.
2

Contextual relevance

Problem
One list for every team ignores group history, occasion, and budget.
Dependency
Phase 1 data quality, group profiles, and confidence scoring.
Experiment
Inferred context plus one question versus today's filters.
Success threshold
Completed orders per team up 2% relative, faster time to first relevant option, one question or fewer per session.
3

Exploration and merchandising

Problem
Teams repeat the same few caterers while new caterers, collections, and items stay hidden.
Dependency
Phase 2 relevance, quality gates, merchandising controls, and menu and item data.
Experiment
The exploratory pick test below, then collections and item suggestions.
Success threshold
Positive incremental completed orders per team, every guardrail inside its margin, new caterer trial up.
Thresholds are illustrative. I would set them with Data before launch.
Metrics

What I would measure, and one experiment I would run first.

North star
Discovery sessions that end in an on time, issue free order.
Conversion alone can be gamed by pushing familiar picks, so only well delivered orders count.
Discovery quality
  • Inference coverage
  • Questions per session, and drop off
  • Time to first relevant option
Recommendation quality
  • Recommendation to order conversion
  • Top three acceptance (diagnostic)
  • Explore versus familiar mix
Business outcomes
  • Search to order conversion
  • Completed orders per team
  • AOV and cart abandonment
  • New caterer trial, repeat ordering, first order conversion for new teams
Guardrails
  • On time rate
  • Issue, refund, and cancellation rates
  • Satisfaction
  • Dietary mismatches: zero tolerance
Worked example

An exploratory pick in the top three

Hypothesis
For recurring teams, replacing the third recommendation with a quality gated exploratory caterer increases completed orders per team, because it reduces repeat fatigue without adding delivery risk.
Design

Team level, not session level

  • Randomize by team, 50/50
  • About four weeks, sized with Data, for example for a 2% lift
  • Read week 1 separately to catch novelty effects
Eligible audience

Who is in

  • Recurring teams: 3+ completed orders in 90 days, in markets with enough vetted new caterers
  • Excluded: first order users, approved vendor lists, very large events
Primary outcome

Incremental completed orders

  • Completed orders per eligible team over 28 days, treatment minus control
  • Completed means delivered, not canceled
  • Clicks and top three acceptance explain the result. They do not decide it.
Guardrails

Must not get worse

  • On time, issue, refund, and cancellation rates
  • Satisfaction and AOV
  • Dietary mismatch: zero tolerance, immediate pause
  • Margins agreed before launch
Key segments

Pre registered

  • Team size and order frequency
  • Occasion, such as client meeting versus team lunch
  • Dietary complexity and new caterer supply density
Rollout decision rule

Decided before we look

  • Ship to all if the lift is positive (95% interval above zero) and guardrails hold
  • Ship to a segment if only a pre registered segment wins
  • Iterate if a guardrail slips. Stop if flat or negative.
  • Ramp 5%, 25%, 50%, 100%, with a 5% holdout for 8 weeks
Sample sizes and thresholds are illustrative. I would set them with Data and Research.

How I would approach it

  • Start with jobs, not role names. Interview admins, orderers, and finance contacts on who orders, approves, and pays today, including shared logins.
  • Default to least privilege. New users get the smallest useful role, and Admins grant more.
  • Ship login first, roles second. Login is contained, so it ships as a quick win that unblocks the conversion funnel. Meanwhile I build the more complex role structure with partner teams across Engineering, Design, Data, Support, and Finance. Roles then roll out behind a flag to a few accounts, with scope and approvals added in steps.
  • Measure login: success rate and time to first order for invited users, plus its effect on the discovery metrics: time to first relevant option and cart abandonment before a search even starts.
  • Measure roles: share of orders needing approval, approval turnaround, access related support contacts, and the drop in shared logins.
Functional requirements I would start with (10, tap to expand)
FR1
Sign in, email first
  • Enter a work email, then the next step adapts
  • Company SSO for recognized domains, otherwise password or a one time code
  • Returning users skip straight to their preferred method
  • Tied to discovery metrics: fewer steps to sign in shortens time to first relevant option and reduces cart abandonment before a user triggers a search
FR2
Sign in recovery and safety
  • Clear, specific error messages
  • Self service password reset and code resend
  • MFA required for Admins
  • Sessions expire predictably and never drop a cart in progress
FR3
System roles
  • Four system roles: Admin, Approver, Orderer, Finance viewer
  • Each has a documented permission set
  • Custom roles are out of scope for the first release
FR4
Scoped assignment
  • Assign a role at organization, location, or team scope
  • One person can hold different roles at different scopes
FR5
Permissions
  • Admin: invite and remove users, assign roles, set limits, manage payment
  • Approver: approve or reject over limit orders, see team spend
  • Orderer: browse, build carts, place orders within limits, reorder
  • Finance: invoices and reports only
FR6
Spend controls
  • Per order and per period limits
  • Set by role or by individual
  • Each limit is a hard stop or routes to approval
FR7
Approval workflow
  • Over limit orders go to a named approver with a notification
  • Approver can approve, reject, or edit
  • Requester sees status
  • Near term delivery dates escalate to a backup approver
FR8
Invites and provisioning
  • Invite by email with a role preselected
  • Domain based auto join defaults to the lowest privilege role
  • SSO group to role mapping is a fast follow
FR9
Migration
  • Every existing user is mapped to a default role
  • An Admin reviews the mapping before it takes effect
  • In flight orders and recurring meal programs keep working
FR10
Enforcement and audit
  • Permissions enforced server side on web and app
  • Interface hides unavailable actions and explains why
  • Role, limit, and approval changes are written to an audit log

How I would approach it

  • Start with the questions people ask today. Review support contacts and interview orderers on where is my order, late arrivals, and last minute headcount changes.
  • Tracking first. Every status must come from a real event, such as restaurant confirmation, pickup, or delivery, never a timer.
  • Edit until the cutoff, quick add after it. Main items lock when the kitchen starts. Quick add covers the rest without disrupting the kitchen.
  • Quick add is opt in for restaurants. Ready made items only, with caps, so a late add on never risks the main order.
  • Measure: where is my order contacts, on time rate, changes made before cutoff, quick add attach rate, and AOV.
Functional requirements I would start with (8, tap to expand)
FR1
Order status model
  • Four customer statuses: Processing, Preparing, Out for delivery, Delivered
  • Each status is set by a restaurant or delivery event, with a timestamp
  • Statuses never move backward, and exceptions such as Delayed show separately
FR2
Tracking view
  • One place to see status, ETA window, restaurant, items, and delivery contact
  • Available on web and app, with a shareable link for teammates
  • Access follows role: Orderers see their orders, Admins see the whole account, Finance sees receipts only
FR3
Notifications
  • Text, email, and push at each status change, with per user preferences
  • Proactive alert when the ETA slips past the delivery window
  • Delivered confirmation with a one tap report an issue
FR4
Edit before cutoff
  • Editable: item quantities, item swaps, headcount, dietary selections, delivery time, and instructions
  • Each restaurant's cutoff is shown with a live countdown
  • Price change shown before saving, and spend limits and approvals re-checked on every edit
FR5
Cutoff handling
  • At cutoff, main items lock and the restaurant is notified once with the final order
  • After cutoff, the edit screen explains what is locked and offers Quick add
  • Cancellation follows the restaurant's policy, shown up front
FR6
Quick add
  • Restaurants opt in and choose ready made add ons, such as drinks, cookies, and sides
  • Caps per order on item count and value, with stock and delivery capacity checks
  • Add ons are charged to the same payment method and tied to the same delivery
  • Restaurant confirms or declines, and the orderer sees the result in the tracking view
FR7
Restaurant side
  • Restaurants update status in one tap, or through their existing integration
  • Changes and quick adds appear on the ticket with a clear diff
  • Restaurants can pause quick add for busy periods
FR8
Audit and support
  • Every status change, edit, and quick add is logged with who and when
  • Support sees the same timeline the customer sees
  • Failed status updates fall back to a manual check with the restaurant
